Set a realistic total budget

If you are shopping with a $5,000–$10,000 purchase budget, decide whether that amount is the advertised vehicle price or your complete out-the-door limit. Taxes, registration and itemized dealer fees can change what you can afford. No vehicle availability at this budget is promised.

What to prioritize at this budget

Compare smaller cars and higher-mileage older vehicles; condition and documented maintenance should lead your shortlist. Leave room for immediate repairs, tires and registration.

Condition can outweigh a lower asking price

At this budget, compare what each actual vehicle needs immediately: tires, brakes, fluid leaks, warning-light diagnosis and overdue maintenance. Ask for a written inspection summary. A vehicle near the bottom of your price range can cost more overall if essential repairs have been deferred.

Check the title before making a deposit

Ask whether the title is clear, branded or subject to a lien, and request the VIN before you travel. Discuss whether an independent inspection is allowed and who pays for it. If financing is needed, ask whether the lender accepts that particular vehicle’s age, mileage and title status; an inquiry is not an approval.
